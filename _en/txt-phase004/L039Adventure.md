# 1. Do you like adventure stories? Why?
> Words may be used:
> exciting
> interesting
> boring
> imagination
> experience
Yes, I really like adventure stories—they’re far from **boring** and always draw me in. 
是的，我真的很喜欢冒险故事——它们远非无聊，总是能吸引我。

First, they’re incredibly **exciting**: whether it’s characters exploring hidden temples, sailing through stormy seas, or solving ancient mysteries, the tense, unpredictable plots keep me on the edge of my seat, making every chapter feel like a new thrill.  
首先，它们令人难以置信地激动人心：无论是人物探索隐藏的寺庙，在波涛汹涌的大海中航行，还是解开古老的谜团，紧张、不可预测的情节都让我感到紧张，让每一章都感觉像是一种新的刺激。

Second, they’re deeply **interesting** because they spark my **imagination**. Adventure stories often take place in vivid, unknown worlds (like a magical jungle or a distant planet) or follow unique quests, which let me “escape” the ordinary and picture myself in those extraordinary scenarios.  
其次，它们非常有趣，因为它们 激发(spark/stimulate)  了我的想象力。冒险故事通常发生在生动、未知的世界（如魔法丛林或遥远的星球），或者遵循独特的任务，这让我“逃离”了平凡，想象自己处于那些非同寻常的场景中。  

Most importantly, they let me “gain” new **experiences** indirectly. Through the characters’ journeys—overcoming fears, working with friends, or discovering new things—I feel like I’m learning about courage, resilience, and the world in a fun, engaging way. All these reasons make adventure stories one of my favorite genres.
最重要的是，它们让我间接地“获得”了新的**体验**。通过角色的旅程——克服恐惧、与朋友合作或发现新事物——我觉得我正在以一种有趣、引人入胜的方式学习勇气、韧性和世界。所有这些原因使冒险故事成为我最喜欢的类型之一。

# 2. Why do people go on adventures?
> Words may be used:
> day-to-day life
> tired of
> seek
> alive
> attractive

People go on adventures primarily because they grow **tired of** the unchanging rhythm of **day-to-day life**. 
Most days follow a predictable loop: waking up at the same time, commuting to work or school, finishing routine tasks, and returning home—this repetition can gradually make life feel flat and uninspiring. 
Adventures, by contrast, shatter this monotony: a weekend hike in an undiscovered forest, a road trip to a small coastal town, or even trying a new extreme activity like rock climbing introduces novelty that rekindles enthusiasm, offering a break from the “same old” routine.  
人们冒险主要是因为他们厌倦了日常生活中一成不变的节奏。
大多数日子都遵循一个可预测的循环：在同一时间醒来，上下班或上学，完成日常任务，然后回家——这种重复会逐渐让生活变得平淡乏味。
相比之下，冒险打破了这种单调：周末在一片未被发现的森林徒步旅行，去一个沿海小镇的公路旅行，甚至尝试攀岩等新的极限活动，都会带来新奇感，重新点燃热情，让人从“旧日”的例行公事中解脱出来。  

Another core reason is that people **seek** to feel genuinely **alive**. 
Adventures push individuals beyond their comfort zones: navigating a tricky trail, adapting to unexpected weather during a camping trip, or communicating with locals in a foreign language forces them to stay present and engaged. 
Overcoming these small challenges sparks intense emotions—excitement, pride, and a sharp sense of being “in the moment”—that routine life rarely provides. 
This intensity makes people feel more connected to their own capabilities and the world, awakening a vividness that feels far more real than daily chores. 
另一个核心原因是人们**寻求**真正（Truly）**活着**。
冒险会让人超越自己的舒适区：在一条棘手的小径上航行，在露营旅行中适应意外的天气，或者用外语与当地人交流，都会迫使他们保持专注和参与。
克服这些小挑战会引发强烈的情绪——兴奋、自豪和“当下”的敏锐感——这是日常生活很少提供的。
这种强度使人们感到与自己的能力和世界更加紧密相连，唤醒了一种比日常琐事更真实的生动感。  

Moreover, the unknown nature of adventures is deeply **attractive**. The chance to discover something new—a hidden waterfall, a community’s unique traditions, or even a new side of oneself—drives people to step out of familiarity. Adventures don’t just create memorable stories; they satisfy curiosity and add depth to life, turning ordinary existence into something richer and more meaningful.
此外，冒险的未知性质非常吸引人。发现新事物的机会——一个隐藏的瀑布、一个社区独特的传统，甚至是自己的新一面——驱使人们走出熟悉。冒险不仅仅创造难忘的故事；它们满足了好奇心，为生活增添了深度，将平凡的存在变成了更丰富、更有意义的东西。

# 3. Is danger a necessary part of adventure?
> Words may be used:
> so long as
> event/activity/journey
> strange
> feeling
> unusual
Danger is not a necessary part of adventure—what defines an adventure is more about breaking from the ordinary than facing risk. An adventure can be any **event, activity, or journey** that takes someone out of their familiar routine, even if it has no hint of danger.  
危险不是冒险的必要组成部分——定义冒险的更多的是打破常规，而不是面对风险。冒险可以是任何**事件、活动或旅程**，让人摆脱熟悉的日常生活，即使没有危险的迹象。  

For example, exploring a **strange** neighborhood in a new city, trying an **unusual** craft like pottery for the first time, or taking a solo train trip to a small town with no fixed plans all count as adventures. These experiences don’t involve danger, but they still bring the core of adventure: novelty and a fresh **feeling** of discovery.  
例如，在一个新城市探索一个奇怪的社区，第一次尝试一种不同寻常的陶器工艺，或者独自坐火车去一个没有固定计划的小镇，都可以算作冒险。这些经历不涉及危险，但它们仍然带来了冒险的核心：新奇和一种新的发现感。  

**So long as** an experience pushes you beyond your daily comfort zone, sparks curiosity, or lets you encounter something new, it qualifies as an adventure. Danger might add excitement for some, but it’s never a requirement. Many people seek adventures precisely to feel alive through new, safe experiences—not to risk harm. Thus, danger is optional, not essential, to what makes an adventure meaningful.
**只要**一次经历让你超越日常舒适区，激发好奇心，或者让你遇到新的东西，它就有资格成为一次冒险。危险可能会让一些人感到兴奋，但这从来不是必须的。许多人寻求冒险正是为了通过新的、安全的体验来感受活力，而不是冒着受伤的风险。因此，对于使冒险有意义的事情来说，危险是可选的，而不是必不可少的。

# 4. Do you consider yourself an adventurous person? Why?
> Words may be used:
> willing
> afraid
> ability
> satisfied with
> risk
I don’t consider myself an overly adventurous person, but I’m far from someone who avoids stepping out of my comfort zone entirely.  
我不认为自己是一个过于冒险的人，但我远非一个完全避免走出舒适区的人。  

First, I’m **willing** to try new things that don’t involve high **risk**—like exploring a lesser-known local park I’ve never visited, learning a simple new skill (such as making hand-brewed coffee), or traveling to a nearby town I’ve only heard about. These small “adventures” let me experience something **unusual** without feeling overwhelmed, and they bring a fresh sense of joy that routine life sometimes lacks.  

首先，我**愿意**尝试不涉及高**风险**的新事物——比如探索一个我从未去过的鲜为人知的当地公园，学习一项简单的新技能（比如煮手工咖啡），或者去附近一个我只听说过的城镇旅行。这些小小的“冒险”让我体验了一些不同寻常的事情，而不会感到不知所措，它们带来了日常生活有时缺乏的新鲜快乐。  

However, I am **afraid** of taking big risks that could harm my safety or stability—like extreme sports (such as bungee jumping) or unplanned long trips to remote areas. I value comfort and predictability to some extent, and I don’t feel the need to chase intense thrills to feel fulfilled.  

然而，我害怕承担可能损害我的安全或稳定性的大风险，比如极限运动（如蹦极）或计划外的偏远地区长途旅行。在某种程度上，我重视舒适性和可预测性，我不觉得有必要追求强烈的刺激来获得满足感。  

Also, I’m generally **satisfied with** small, low-stakes adventures that match my current **ability** to adapt. I don’t need grand, dangerous journeys to feel “alive”; even a short walk in a new neighborhood or a taste of an unfamiliar dish feels like a little adventure to me. So while I’m not a bold adventurer, I’m open to gentle, manageable experiences that add novelty to life.

此外，我通常对与我目前的适应能力相匹配的小型、低风险的冒险感到满意。我不需要伟大而危险的旅程来感受“活着”；对我来说，即使是在一个新社区走一小段路，或者品尝一道不熟悉的菜肴，也感觉像是一次小冒险。所以，虽然我不是一个大胆的冒险家，但我对温和、可控的体验持开放态度，这些体验为生活增添了新鲜感。

# 5. Would you consider picking up strangers on the road?
> Words may be used:
> depend on
> situation
> most of the time
> in danger
> trust

I wouldn’t easily decide to pick up strangers on the road—it mainly **depend on** the specific **situation**.  
我不会轻易决定在路上接陌生人——这主要取决于**具体的**情况**。  

**Most of the time**, I’d be cautious and avoid it. Strangers are people I know nothing about, and there’s no way to quickly build **trust**; picking them up could put me **in danger** (like facing theft, harm, or other unexpected risks) since I can’t judge their intentions. For example, if I’m driving alone at night on a quiet rural road and a stranger waves for a ride, I’d worry about potential safety threats and choose not to stop.  

**大多数时候**，我会小心避免。陌生人是我一无所知的人，没有办法快速建立**信任**；捡起它们可能会让我**处于危险之中**（比如面临盗窃、伤害或其他意外风险），因为我无法判断他们的意图。例如，如果我晚上独自在一条安静的乡村公路上开车，一个陌生人挥手要搭车，我会担心潜在的安全威胁，选择不停车。  

But in some rare, low-risk situations, I might consider it. Like if I’m with friends in a busy suburban area during the day, and a stranger (say, an elderly person with a heavy bag or a parent with a young kid) clearly needs help getting to a nearby location—such a scenario feels safer, with fewer hidden risks. Even then, I’d keep my guard up, confirm their destination briefly, and make sure my friends are aware.  

但在一些罕见的低风险情况下，我可能会考虑这样做。比如，如果我白天和朋友在繁忙的郊区，一个陌生人（比如，一个背着沉重包的老人或一个带着小孩的父母）显然需要帮助才能到达附近的地点——这种情况感觉更安全，隐藏的风险更小。即便如此，我仍会保持警惕，简要确认他们的目的地，并确保我的朋友们知道。  

Overall, safety comes first, so my choice to pick up a stranger always hinges on how secure the situation feels.

总的来说，安全是第一位的，所以我选择接陌生人总是取决于情况有多安全。

# 6. What’s the most exciting thing you’ve ever done? Please provide a brief answer.
The most exciting thing I’ve ever done was a 2-day hiking trip to a remote mountain valley last year. On the first afternoon, we got lost due to a broken trail marker—darkness was falling, and our phones had no signal. Instead of panicking, we used a paper map (luckily I’d packed one!) to find a small stream, then followed it downhill until we spotted a village. When we finally reached a local family’s home and they offered us hot meals and a place to stay, the mix of relief, pride (that we’d solved the problem ourselves), and the thrill of turning an unexpected detour into an adventure made it feel incredibly exciting. It’s a memory I still laugh about and cherish now.
我做过的最令人兴奋的事情是去年去一个偏远山谷徒步旅行两天。第一天下午，我们因路标断裂而迷路了——夜幕降临，我们的手机没有信号。我们没有惊慌失措，而是用一张纸质地图（幸运的是我带了一张！）找到了一条小溪，然后顺着它往下走，直到我们发现了一个村庄。当我们终于到达当地一个家庭的家，他们为我们提供了热腾腾的饭菜和一个住宿的地方时，解脱、自豪（我们自己解决了这个问题）和将意外的迂回变成冒险的快感让我们感到无比兴奋。这是一段我至今仍笑着珍惜的回忆。

# 7. If you could travel through time and space, what time and place would you choose?Please provide a brief answer.
If I could travel through time and space, I’d choose to visit **17th-century Kyoto, Japan**, during the Edo period. I’ve always been fascinated by how this era balanced tradition and quiet innovation—walking through the wooden streets lined with machiya (traditional townhouses), watching geisha practice tea ceremonies in Gion, or observing craftsmen make ukiyo-e woodblock prints would feel like stepping into a living painting. It’s a time and place where life moved at a gentler pace, rooted in art, ritual, and community, and I’d love to experience that calm, culturally rich world firsthand, far from the noise of modern life.
如果我能穿越时空，我会选择参观江户时代的17世纪日本京都。我一直着迷于这个时代是如何平衡传统和安静的创新的——走过满是町屋（传统联排别墅）的木制街道，看着艺妓在只园练习茶道，或者观察工匠制作浮世绘木版画，感觉就像走进一幅活画。这是一个生活以更温和的节奏发展的时间和地点，植根于艺术、仪式和社区，我很想亲身体验这个平静、文化丰富的世界，远离现代生活的喧嚣。

# 8. Talk about one of your favorite adventure movies or books.Please provide a brief answer.
One of my favorite adventure books is *The Hobbit* by J.R.R. Tolkien. What I love most is how it turns a small, comfort-loving hobbit (Bilbo Baggins) into an unlikely hero—his journey from his cozy hobbit-hole to the Lonely Mountain, facing goblins, a dragon (Smaug), and even finding the One Ring, feels both thrilling and heartfelt. The book balances exciting action (like the escape from the Misty Mountains) with warm moments of friendship (between Bilbo and the dwarves) and quiet growth (Bilbo learning courage isn’t about being fearless, but trying anyway). It’s an adventure that feels personal, making you root for Bilbo every step of the way.
我最喜欢的冒险书之一是J.R.R.托尔金的《霍比特人》。我最喜欢的是它如何把一个小的、喜欢舒适的霍比特人（比尔博·巴金斯饰）变成一个不太可能的英雄——他从舒适的霍比人洞到孤独山的旅程，面对地精、龙（史矛革饰），甚至找到了至尊魔戒，感觉既激动人心又发自内心。这本书平衡了激动人心的动作（比如逃离迷雾山脉）、温暖的友谊时刻（比尔博和矮人之间）和平静的成长（比尔博学习勇气不是无所畏惧，而是无论如何都要尝试）。这是一次感觉很私人的冒险，让你在每一步都支持比尔博。

# 10. Which country would you like to live in other than your own?Please provide a brief answer.
Other than my own country, I’d love to live in **New Zealand** for a while. What draws me most is its perfect mix of peaceful daily life and stunning natural beauty—waking up to views of snow-capped Southern Alps, having easy access to hiking trails in Fiordland National Park, or spending weekends exploring quiet coastal towns like Kaikoura (where you can spot dolphins right from the shore) sounds incredibly calming. Beyond the scenery, I also admire its laid-back, community-focused culture—people often prioritize work-life balance, and there’s a strong respect for the land (rooted in Māori values of *kaitiakitanga*, or guardianship). It feels like a place where I could slow down, connect with nature regularly, and build a gentle, fulfilling routine.

除了我自己的国家，我很想在新西兰住一段时间。最吸引我的是它将宁静的日常生活和令人惊叹的自然美景完美地结合在一起——醒来时可以欣赏到白雪皑皑的南阿尔卑斯山的景色，可以轻松地前往峡湾国家公园的远足径，或者在周末探索凯库拉等安静的沿海城镇（在那里你可以从岸边看到海豚）听起来非常平静。除了风景，我还欣赏它悠闲、以社区为中心的文化——人们经常优先考虑工作与生活的平衡，并且对土地有着强烈的尊重（植根于毛利人的*kaitiakitanga*或监护价值观）。这感觉就像是一个我可以放慢脚步，定期与大自然联系，建立一个温和、充实的日常生活的地方。
