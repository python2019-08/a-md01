# 1. Should we show our emotions when we're angry?
> Words may be used:
> situation
> express
> feeling
> argue
> control
Whether we should show our anger depends on the **situation** and whether we can do it with **control**—it’s not about hiding anger entirely, but expressing it in a way that doesn’t hurt others.  

我们是否应该表达我们的愤怒取决于**情况**以及我们是否能够**控制**它——这并不是要完全隐藏愤怒，而是以一种不伤害他人的方式表达它。  

If we bottle up angry **feeling**s, they might build up and lead to a bigger, unplanned outburst later (like starting an unnecessary **argue**). So it’s healthier to **express** anger when it matters—for example, if someone crosses a boundary, calmly saying “I felt upset when you did X” helps them understand your perspective. But we need to stay in control: yelling, blaming, or lashing out only makes the situation worse, as it makes the other person defensive instead of willing to listen.  
如果我们把愤怒的情绪压抑在心里，它们可能会积聚起来，导致更大的、无计划的爆发（比如开始不必要的争吵）。因此，在重要的时候表达愤怒更健康——例如，如果有人越界，冷静地说“你做X事时我感到很沮丧”有助于他们理解你的观点。但我们需要保持控制：大喊大叫、责备或猛烈抨击只会让情况变得更糟，因为这会让对方产生防御心理，而不是愿意倾听。  


In short, we shouldn’t suppress anger, but we should choose *how* and *when* to show it—matching our expression to the situation, keeping our cool, and focusing on sharing our feelings rather than starting a fight.  
简而言之，我们不应该压抑愤怒，但我们应该选择*如何*和*何时*展示它 - 将我们的表达与情况相匹配，保持冷静，并专注于分享我们的感受，而不是开始战斗。

# 2. Do women show their emotions more than men? why?
> Words may be used:
> bring up
> weak/strong
> sensitive
> teach
> deal with
It’s not that women *naturally* show emotions more than men, but rather that societal norms and upbringing often shape how each gender is taught to express feelings.  
这并不是说女性“自然”比男性更善于表达情感，而是社会规范和教养往往塑造了每种性别被教导如何表达情感的方式。  

From a young age, many cultures **teach** boys to suppress “softer” emotions (like sadness or vulnerability) and associate emotional restraint with being “strong”—they may be told not to cry or “brush off” upsetting feelings, which makes them less likely to **bring up** or outwardly show their emotions. In contrast, girls are often seen as more “**sensitive**” (a label that’s sometimes unfair, but deeply ingrained) and face fewer barriers to expressing how they feel—they might be encouraged to talk about their sadness, anger, or joy openly, without being judged for “not being strong enough.”  
从很小的时候起，许多文化就**教导**男孩压抑“软弱”的情绪（如悲伤或脆弱），并将情绪克制与“坚强”联系起来——他们可能会被告知不要哭泣或“摆脱”令人沮丧的情绪，这使得他们不太可能**表达**或外露自己的情绪。相比之下，女孩往往被视为更“**敏感**”（这个标签有时不公平，但根深蒂固），在表达自己的感受时面临的障碍较少——她们可能会被鼓励公开谈论自己的悲伤、愤怒或喜悦，而不会被评判为“不够坚强”


This doesn’t mean men feel emotions less deeply, or that all women are highly expressive—individual differences matter a lot. But the way society teaches genders to **deal with** emotions creates a pattern where women often feel more comfortable showing their feelings publicly, while men may keep them hidden to fit the “strong” stereotype. It’s a cultural influence, not a difference in the *amount* of emotion felt.  

这并不意味着男性对情感的感受程度较低，也不意味着所有女性都善于表达情感——个体差异很重要。但是社会教导性别如何处理情感的方式形成了一种模式，在这种模式下，女性在公开场合表达自己的情感时往往感觉更舒适，而男性则可能为了符合“坚强”的刻板印象而隐藏自己的情感。这是一种文化影响，而不是情感感受的“数量”差异。

# 3. what are the physical effects of anger?
> Words may be used:
> produce
> harmful
> disease
> anxiety
> damage
Anger, when frequent or intense, can trigger significant physical effects that harm the body over time, as our bodies respond to it like a stress signal.  
愤怒，当频繁或强烈时，会随着时间的推移引发对身体有害的显著生理效应，因为我们的身体会像对待压力信号一样对其做出反应。  

First, anger makes the body **produce** a surge of stress hormones (like cortisol and adrenaline). These hormones raise blood pressure and heart rate temporarily—but if anger is chronic, this constant pressure can **damage** the heart and blood vessels, increasing the risk of heart-related **disease** (such as hypertension or even heart attacks) over time. 
首先，愤怒会使身体产生大量压力激素（如皮质醇和肾上腺素）。这些激素会暂时升高血压和心率，但如果愤怒是长期的，这种持续的压力会损害心脏和血管，随着时间的推移，增加心脏病（如高血压甚至心脏病发作）的风险。  

Second, prolonged anger weakens the immune system. The ongoing release of those stress hormones suppresses immune cell function, making the body more vulnerable to illnesses—from common colds to more serious health issues.  
其次，长期生气会削弱免疫系统。这些压力激素的持续释放会抑制免疫细胞的功能，使身体更容易患病，从普通感冒到更严重的健康问题。  

Additionally, anger often links to **anxiety** or tension, which can cause physical discomfort right away: muscle tightness (especially in the shoulders, neck, or jaw), headaches, or even digestive problems (like stomachaches or acid reflux). Over time, these physical tensions can become chronic, leading to long-term pain or discomfort. 
此外，愤怒往往与焦虑或紧张有关，这会立即引起身体不适：肌肉紧张（尤其是肩膀、颈部或下巴）、头痛，甚至消化问题（如胃痛或胃酸反流）。随着时间的推移，这些身体紧张会变成慢性疾病，导致长期疼痛或不适。  

In short, while occasional anger is normal, frequent or unmanaged anger releases **harmful** physical responses that chip away at overall health—affecting the heart, immune system, and even daily physical comfort.  
简而言之，虽然偶尔生气是正常的，但频繁或无法控制的愤怒会释放出有害的身体反应，损害整体健康，影响心脏、免疫系统，甚至日常的身体舒适度。

# 4.Can anger be good for us?
> Words may be used:
> motivate
> push
> right away
> sign
> find out
Yes, anger can be good for us—when we use it as a signal rather than letting it take over. It’s not the anger itself that’s helpful, but how we respond to it.  
是的，愤怒对我们来说是有益的——当我们把它当作一个信号而不是让它控制我们的时候。不是愤怒本身有益，而是我们如何应对它。

First, anger acts as a **sign** that something is wrong: it alerts us to boundaries being crossed (like being treated unfairly), unmet needs (like feeling ignored), or situations that feel unjust. This signal helps us **find out** what matters to us—for example, if we’re angry about a friend canceling plans last minute, it might show we value respect and reliability.  
首先，愤怒是一种**信号**，表明有些事情出了问题：它提醒我们界限被跨越（如受到不公平对待）、需求未得到满足（如感到被忽视）或情况不公。这个信号帮助我们**发现**对我们来说重要的事情——例如，如果我们因为朋友最后一刻取消计划而生气，这可能表明我们重视尊重和可靠性。  


Second, anger can **motivate** us to take positive action. Instead of lashing out, we can let that frustration **push** us to fix the problem: if we’re angry about unfair treatment at work, we might gather courage to speak up calmly; if we’re upset about a personal goal we’re not meeting, anger can fuel us to try harder. Unlike impulsive reactions that happen **right away**, this motivated action turns anger into a tool for change.  
其次，愤怒可以**激励**我们采取积极的行动。与其大发雷霆，我们不如让这种挫败感**推动**我们去解决问题：如果我们对工作中受到的不公平待遇感到愤怒，我们可能会鼓起勇气冷静地发声；如果我们对自己没有达到的个人目标感到沮丧，愤怒可以激励我们更加努力。与**立即**发生的冲动反应不同，这种有动机的行动将愤怒转化为改变的工具。  


Of course, this only works if we don’t let anger spiral into aggression or holding grudges. When we use it to understand our needs and drive positive steps, anger becomes a useful emotion—one that helps us stand up for ourselves and grow.  
当然，只有当我们不让愤怒演变成攻击或怨恨时，这种方法才有效。当我们用它来理解我们的需求并推动积极的步骤时，愤怒就变成了一种有用的情绪——一种帮助我们为自己挺身而出并成长的情绪。

# 5. How should we deal with anger?
> Words may be used:
> fight
> focus on
> breathe
> reason
> understand
Dealing with anger well means calming yourself first and then addressing the root of the feeling—instead of letting it lead to a **fight** or regretful words.  
妥善处理愤怒意味着首先要冷静下来，然后解决情绪的根源，而不是让它导致争吵或后悔的话语。  

First, pause and **breathe** when anger hits. Taking slow, deep breaths (inhaling for 4 seconds, holding for 4, exhaling for 6) helps calm the body’s stress response—slowing a racing heart and quieting the urge to react **right away**. This small step gives you space to think instead of acting on impulse.  

首先，当愤怒袭来时，暂停一下，**深呼吸**。缓慢而深长的呼吸（吸气4秒，屏息4秒，呼气6秒）有助于平息身体的应激反应——减缓心跳加速，平息**立即**反应的冲动。这一小步能给你思考的空间，而不是冲动行事。  

Next, try to **understand** why you’re angry. Ask yourself: What exactly triggered this feeling? Is it a boundary being crossed, a need not being met, or frustration about a situation? This helps you see anger as a signal (not just a “bad” emotion) and keeps you from lashing out at the wrong thing or person.  
接下来，试着**理解**你为什么会生气。问问自己：到底是什么引发了这种感觉？是界限被跨越，需求未得到满足，还是对某种情况的沮丧？这有助于你将愤怒视为一种信号（而不仅仅是一种“坏”情绪），并防止你对错误的事情或人发火。  


Then, **focus on** solving the problem, not “winning.” Instead of rehashing what made you mad, use **reason** to think: What do I need to feel better? Can I talk to the person calmly about this? For example, if a friend forgot a plan, saying “I felt let down when we didn’t meet up—could we reschedule soon?” works better than yelling “You always forget about me!”  
然后，**专注于**解决问题，而不是“获胜”。不要重复让你生气的事情，而是用**理智**思考：我需要什么才能感觉更好？我能冷静地和这个人谈谈这件事吗？例如，如果一个朋友忘记了计划，说“当我们没有见面时，我感到失望——我们能否尽快重新安排？”比大喊“你总是忘记我！”要好得多


Finally, if you’re too upset to talk, step away temporarily (go for a walk, listen to music) to cool down. The goal isn’t to ignore anger, but to handle it in a way that respects both yourself and others—turning frustration into something you can manage, not something that manages you.  

最后，如果你感到太沮丧而不想说话，暂时离开一下（去散步，听音乐）来冷静下来。目标不是忽视愤怒，而是以一种尊重自己和别人的方式来处理它——将沮丧转化为你可以控制的东西，而不是让它控制你。
