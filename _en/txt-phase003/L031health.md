# 1. How can we stay healthy?
> Words may be used:
> eat
> proper
> balance
> exercise
> sleep

Staying healthy is about cultivating consistent, mindful habits that nurture both body and mind, and it revolves around finding a **balance** in key areas of daily life.  

First, **eat**ing well is foundational. Opting for **proper** nutrition means focusing on whole, unprocessed foods—plenty of fruits, vegetables, lean proteins, and whole grains—while limiting sugary snacks, excessive salt, and unhealthy fats. Portion control matters too; overeating, even of healthy foods, can disrupt energy levels and weight. A balanced diet fuels the body, supports immunity, and keeps organs functioning smoothly.  

Regular **exercise** is equally vital. It doesn’t have to be intense—activities like walking, cycling, or yoga for 30 minutes most days can boost cardiovascular health, strengthen muscles, and release endorphins (the “feel-good” hormones). Mixing cardio with strength training helps maintain flexibility and stamina, ensuring the body stays resilient.  

Adequate **sleep** is often overlooked but critical. Adults need 7–9 hours nightly to allow the body to repair cells, consolidate memories, and regulate hormones that control hunger and mood. Poor sleep weakens immunity, impairs focus, and increases the risk of chronic issues, so prioritizing a consistent sleep schedule—avoiding screens before bed and creating a calm environment—supports overall health.  

Finally, **balance** ties it all together. It’s not about strict diets or exhausting workouts, but moderation: enjoying treats occasionally, resting when tired, and managing stress through hobbies or meditation. By combining **proper** eating, regular exercise, sufficient sleep, and a balanced approach to daily life, we can build a sustainable foundation for long-term health.

# 2. Do you usually get enough sleep? Why?
> Words may be used:
> important
> energetic
> busy
> instead of
> fall asleep
> 
I try to get enough sleep, though it isn’t always easy—especially on busy days. I know sleep is incredibly **important** because it keeps me **energetic** during the day, helping me focus on work, exercise, and daily tasks. When I skimp on sleep, I feel sluggish and less productive, so I prioritize it as much as I can.  

On most nights, I aim for 7–8 hours. I avoid screens an hour before bed to help me **fall asleep** faster, and I stick to a consistent schedule. However, there are weeks when deadlines pile up or plans run late, making it hard to wind down. Even then, I try not to replace sleep with other activities—**instead of** staying up to scroll through my phone or finish one more task, I remind myself that rest will let me tackle things better the next day.  

Overall, I value sleep too much to neglect it regularly. Its role in keeping me sharp and healthy makes it non-negotiable, even when life gets busy.

# 3. Do you take medicines when you are sick? Why or why not?
> Words may be used:
> help
> recover
> harmful
> taste
> swallow
> 

Yes, I do take medicines when I’m sick, but I’m careful to use them appropriately. Medicines can **help** the body fight off illness and speed up **recover**, especially for conditions like infections or severe symptoms that the immune system might struggle with alone. For example, antibiotics can clear bacterial infections, and pain relievers can ease discomfort, making it easier to rest and heal.  

是的，我生病时确实会吃药，但我会小心地适当使用。药物可以**帮助**身体抵抗疾病，加快**恢复**，特别是对于感染或免疫系统可能独自应对的严重症状等情况。例如，抗生素可以清除细菌感染，止痛药可以缓解不适，使其更容易休息和愈合。  

That said, I’m mindful that some medicines can be **harmful** if misused—taking the wrong dose or using them for minor issues might disrupt the body’s natural balance. I always follow a doctor’s advice to avoid such risks.  

也就是说，我注意到，如果滥用，一些药物可能是有害的——服用错误的剂量或将其用于轻微的问题可能会破坏身体的自然平衡。我总是听从医生的建议来避免这种风险。  

As for the **taste** or difficulty to **swallow**, they’re minor inconveniences compared to the benefits. Even if a pill is bitter or hard to get down, the goal of getting better quickly matters more.  

至于**味道**或**吞咽困难**，与好处相比，它们只是轻微的不便。即使一颗药丸很苦或很难下咽，快速好转的目标也更重要。  

In short, medicines are a useful tool when needed: they aid recovery, and with proper use, their benefits far outweigh any temporary downsides like taste or swallowing struggles.

简而言之，药物在需要时是一种有用的工具：它们有助于恢复，如果使用得当，它们的好处远远超过任何暂时的缺点，如味觉或吞咽困难。

# 4. How can a person stop smoking?
> Words may be used:
> reason
> prepare
> replace
> avoid
> reward
Stopping smoking is a challenging but rewarding journey, and it often starts with intentional steps. Here’s how a person can approach it, using key strategies tied to the words provided:  
戒烟是一个具有挑战性但有益的旅程，它通常始于有意识的步骤。以下是一个人如何使用与所提供单词相关的关键策略来处理它：

First, identifying a strong **reason** is crucial. Whether it’s improving health, protecting family from secondhand smoke, or saving money, a clear, personal motivation acts as a anchor during tough moments. For example, wanting to be more active with kids or reducing the risk of lung disease can fuel determination when cravings hit.  

首先，确定一个强有力的**原因**至关重要。无论是改善健康、保护家庭免受二手烟的侵害，还是省钱，在艰难时刻，一个明确的个人动机都会起到锚定作用。例如，当渴望袭来时，想和孩子们更活跃地相处或降低患肺病的风险可以增强决心。  

Next, **prepare** in advance. This means setting a quit date to mentally gear up, removing cigarettes, lighters, and ashtrays from home and work to eliminate triggers, and even telling friends or family for support. Preparing also involves learning about cravings—knowing they usually peak in 5–10 minutes can help someone ride them out.  

接下来，**提前准备**。这意味着设定一个戒烟日期，在精神上做好准备，从家里和工作中取出香烟、打火机和烟灰缸，以消除触发因素，甚至告诉朋友或家人寻求支持。准备还包括了解食欲——知道它们通常在5-10分钟内达到峰值可以帮助人们摆脱它们。  

It’s helpful to **replace** smoking with healthier habits. When the urge strikes, chew gum, snack on nuts, or sip water to keep hands and mouth busy. Taking a short walk or doing deep breathing can also distract from cravings, swapping the smoking ritual with activities that feel refreshing instead of depriving.  

用更健康的习惯取代吸烟是有帮助的。当冲动袭来时，嚼口香糖、吃坚果或喝水，以保持手和嘴的忙碌。短距离散步或深呼吸也可以分散人们对吸烟的渴望，用让人感到清爽而不是剥夺的活动来代替吸烟仪式。  

**Avoid** situations that trigger the habit, at least initially. This might mean skipping bars where smoking was common, or steering clear of old routines like smoking after meals (try brushing teeth instead). Over time, as the habit weakens, these situations become easier to handle without relapsing.  

**避免触发习惯的**情况，至少在最初是这样。这可能意味着跳过吸烟常见的酒吧，或者摆脱饭后吸烟等旧习惯（试着刷牙）。随着时间的推移，随着习惯的减弱，这些情况变得更容易处理而不会复发。  

Finally, **reward** progress to stay motivated. Celebrate small wins—a day without smoking, a week, a month—with treats that feel meaningful: a new book, a massage, or a fun outing. These rewards reinforce the effort and make the journey feel less like a sacrifice.  

最后，**奖励**进步以保持动力。庆祝小小的胜利——不吸烟的一天、一周、一个月——享受有意义的待遇：一本新书、一次按摩或一次有趣的郊游。这些奖励加强了努力，让旅程感觉不像是一种牺牲。  

Quitting smoking isn’t easy, but combining a strong reason, careful preparation, healthy replacements, trigger avoidance, and self-rewards can turn the challenge into a lasting change. The key is to be patient and kind to oneself, as setbacks are normal—but each step forward brings closer to a smoke-free life.

戒烟并不容易，但结合强有力的理由、仔细的准备、健康的替代品、避免触发和自我奖励，可以将挑战转化为持久的改变。关键是要有耐心，善待自己，因为挫折是正常的——但每向前迈出一步，就离无烟生活更近了一步。

# 5. How can we protect our mental health?
> Words may be used:
> positive
> hope
> accept
> relationship
> goal
> 

To protect mental health: 
- Stay **positive** and keep **hope**; 
- **Accept** imperfection; 
- Nurture good **relationships**; 
- Set small, achievable **goals**.
=========
Protecting mental health is a proactive, ongoing process that involves nurturing emotional resilience and fostering healthy connections with oneself and others. Here’s how key practices, tied to the provided words, can support this: 
保护心理健康是一个积极主动、持续的过程，涉及培养情绪弹性，培养与自己和他人的健康联系。以下是与提供的单词相关的关键实践如何支持这一点：


### Cultivate a **positive** mindset  
Focusing on positivity doesn’t mean ignoring hardships—it means training the mind to acknowledge challenges while also seeking out small joys. This could involve keeping a gratitude journal to note daily blessings, reframing negative thoughts (e.g., “I messed up” becomes “I learned something”), or surrounding oneself with uplifting content or people. A positive outlook acts as a buffer against stress, making it easier to navigate life’s ups and downs.  
### 培养**积极**的心态
专注于积极并不意味着忽视困难——这意味着训练大脑在承认挑战的同时，也要寻找小的快乐。这可能涉及写一本感恩日记来记录每天的祝福，重新构建负面想法（例如，“我搞砸了”变成“我学到了一些东西”），或者用令人振奋的内容或人包围自己。积极的态度可以缓冲压力，让你更容易驾驭生活的起伏。  

### Hold onto **hope**  
Hope is the belief that better outcomes are possible, even in difficult times. It can be nurtured by setting small, achievable steps toward improvement—whether it’s “I’ll talk to a friend tomorrow” or “I’ll try a new hobby this week.” Trusting that growth and healing take time, and visualizing a brighter future, helps combat feelings of hopelessness and keeps motivation alive.  
### 坚持**希望**
希望是一种信念，即即使在困难时期，也有可能取得更好的结果。它可以通过设定小的、可实现的改进步骤来培养——无论是“我明天会和朋友谈谈”还是“我本周会尝试一个新的爱好”。相信成长和治愈需要时间，想象一个更光明的未来，有助于对抗绝望感，保持动力。  

### Learn to **accept** what you can’t change  
Mental strain often comes from resisting reality—fixating on past mistakes or worrying about the unknown. Acceptance involves acknowledging emotions (sadness, anger, stress) without judgment, and recognizing that some things are beyond our control. This frees up energy to focus on what *can* be changed, reducing anxiety and fostering inner peace. For example, instead of dwelling on a missed opportunity, accept it and shift focus to the next one.  
### 学会接受你无法改变的事情
精神压力往往来自抵制现实——执着于过去的错误或担心未知。接受意味着不加评判地承认情绪（悲伤、愤怒、压力），并认识到有些事情是我们无法控制的。这可以释放能量，专注于可以改变的事情，减少焦虑，促进内心的平静。例如，与其纠缠于错过的机会，不如接受它，把注意力转移到下一个机会上。  

### Nurture meaningful **relationships**  
Strong connections with family, friends, or community provide emotional support, a sense of belonging, and a safe space to share feelings. Regularly checking in with loved ones, listening actively, and being vulnerable builds trust. Even small interactions—like a heartfelt conversation or a shared laugh—can boost mood and reduce loneliness, a major risk factor for poor mental health.  
### 培养有意义的**关系**
与家人、朋友或社区的紧密联系提供了情感支持、归属感和分享感受的安全空间。定期与亲人联系，积极倾听，脆弱会建立信任。即使是很小的互动，比如真诚的交谈或共同的笑声，也可以改善情绪，减少孤独感，这是心理健康状况不佳的主要危险因素。  

### Set purposeful **goals**  
Goals give life direction and a sense of achievement, which are vital for self-esteem. They don’t need to be grand—aiming to “read for 10 minutes daily” or “take a walk three times a week” can provide structure and a sense of progress. Breaking larger goals into smaller steps makes them less overwhelming, and celebrating each milestone reinforces a sense of capability.  

By combining these practices—fostering positivity, clinging to hope, embracing acceptance, nurturing relationships, and pursuing goals—we create a foundation for robust mental health. It’s not about perfection, but about showing up for ourselves, daily, with kindness and intention.
### 设定有目的的**目标**
目标赋予人生方向和成就感，这对自尊至关重要。它们不需要很宏伟——目标是“每天阅读10分钟”或“每周散步三次”可以提供结构和进步感。将更大的目标分解为更小的步骤会让它们变得不那么压倒性，庆祝每个里程碑会增强一种能力感。  

通过结合这些实践——培养积极性、坚持希望、接受、培养关系和追求目标——我们为健全的心理健康奠定了基础。这不是关于完美，而是关于每天带着善良和意图为自己出现。
