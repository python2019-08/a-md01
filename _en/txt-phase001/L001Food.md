I usually have breakfast around 7:30 a.m. There are several reasons for this.
First of all, I get up early, typically around 7:00 a.m. After getting up, I need some time to freshen up. I wash my face, brush my teeth, and put on some light clothes. This simple routine usually takes about half an hour. By 7:30, I'm ready to refuel my body.
Secondly, I like to do a bit of exercise in the morning.        A short 15-minute workout at home, like some simple stretching and light jogging in place, helps wake me up completely. After exercising, my body is in a state where it craves energy, so having breakfast at 7:30 is perfect to replenish the energy I've used during the workout.
Moreover, I have a habit of morning reading. Before having breakfast, I'll spend around 15 minutes reading some inspiring articles or news. It not only broadens my knowledge but also gets my mind active. Once I'm done with my reading, I feel hungry and it's the right time to sit down and enjoy my breakfast.
Sometimes, I also check my phone briefly to see if there are any important messages or notifications. But this doesn't take long. By 7:30, all these pre - breakfast activities are finished, and I can focus on having a delicious and healthy breakfast to start my day on the right foot. 

# 3. Do you care about the color when you have a meal? 
> Words may be used:
> multiple colors
> white /red / green / black/yellow
> health
> appetite  【英/ˈæpɪtaɪt/美/ˈæpɪtaɪt/n.食欲；胃口；强烈欲望】
> no food coloring【无食用色素】
Yes, I do care about the color when I have a meal. Having multiple colors on my plate is not only visually appealing but also beneficial for my health.
I believe that **a balanced meal** should include foods of different colors. For example, white foods like cauliflower[英/ˈkɒliflaʊə(r)/ 花椰菜] and tofu are rich in nutrients[英/ˈnjuːtrɪənts/营养物]. Cauliflower is a good source of vitamins and fiber, while tofu is an excellent plant - based protein.
Red foods such as tomatoes and strawberries are also important. Tomatoes are full of lycopene, an antioxidant that is good for the heart. Strawberries are rich in vitamin C and add a sweet and refreshing taste to the meal.
Green is a color that represents freshness. Spinach, lettuce, and broccoli are common green vegetables. They are packed with vitamins, minerals, and chlorophyll, which are essential for our body's normal functions.
Black foods like black beans and black sesame are often overlooked but are very nutritious. Black beans are high in protein and fiber, and black sesame is good for hair and skin health.
Yellow foods, including bananas and corn, provide energy. Bananas are a great source of potassium, and corn is rich in carbohydrates.
When I see a meal with a variety of colors, it really boosts my appetite. It makes the food look more delicious and enticing[英/ɪnˈtaɪsɪŋ/美/ɪnˈtaɪsɪŋ/adj.诱人的]. And I always try to choose natural foods without any food coloring. This way, I can ensure that the colors come from the natural nutrients in the food, which is much healthier. So, color plays an important role in my meals as it combines both health and a good eating experience.
 



# 4. Do you care about food nutrition[英/njuˈtrɪʃn/ 营养]? Why?
> Words may be used:
> fats
> protein
> vitamins
> carbohydrates
> water

Yes, I definitely care about food nutrition. The reason lies in the fact that a well-balanced intake of various nutrients is fundamental for maintaining good health.
First and foremost, protein is of great significance. It is the building block of our body. It helps in the growth, repair, and maintenance of tissues. Foods like lean meats, fish, eggs, and beans are rich in protein. Consuming an adequate amount of protein ensures that my muscles, skin, and organs function properly.
Carbohydrates are also essential. They are the main source of energy for our body. Complex carbohydrates such as whole grains provide a slow - releasing form of energy, keeping me active throughout the day. Simple carbohydrates, found in fruits, can give a quick energy boost when needed.
Fats, although often seen as something to avoid, are actually necessary in our diet. Healthy fats, like those in avocados, nuts, and olive oil, are important for brain function, hormone production, and the absorption of fat - soluble vitamins.
Vitamins play a crucial role in numerous bodily functions. For example, vitamin C, found in citrus fruits, is an antioxidant that helps boost the immune system. Vitamin D, which can be obtained from sunlight and some fortified foods, is important for bone health.
Water is the most basic yet vital nutrient. It is involved in almost every bodily process, from digestion to circulation. Staying well - hydrated helps maintain the balance of bodily fluids, regulate body temperature, and flush out toxins.
By caring about food nutrition and ensuring a proper intake of fats, protein, vitamins, carbohydrates, and water, I can enhance my overall well - being, prevent diseases, and have the energy to lead an active and fulfilling life.
