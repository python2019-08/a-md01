# 1. Do you think the Internet is the best invention ever?
> Words may be used:
> history
> human 
> information
> communication
> business

I don’t think the Internet is definitively the “best” invention ever—since “best” depends on how we weigh impacts across human history—but it’s undoubtedly one of the most transformative. It has revolutionized how humans access information (turning vast knowledge into instant, global resources), redefined communication (connecting people across continents in seconds, beyond distance), and reshaped business (enabling online trade, remote work, and global markets that were once unthinkable). While inventions like the wheel or electricity laid foundational groundwork for human progress, the Internet’s unique power to unify information, communication, and business on a global scale makes it a standout in modern history—even if “best” is too subjective to claim.
我不认为互联网绝对是有史以来“最好”的发明——因为“最好”取决于我们如何衡量人类历史上的影响——但它无疑是最具变革性的发明之一。它彻底改变了人类获取信息的方式（将大量知识转化为即时的全球资源），重新定义了沟通方式（在几秒钟内、远距离连接各大洲的人们），并重塑了业务（实现了曾经不可想象的在线交易、远程工作和全球市场）。虽然轮子或电力等发明为人类进步奠定了基础，但互联网在全球范围内统一信息、通信和商业的独特力量使其在现代历史上脱颖而出——即使“最好”的说法过于主观。

# 3. what are the disadvantages of the Internet for children?
> Words may be used:
> harmful
> eyes
> appropriate
> lose interest in
> studying
>   
The Internet has several notable disadvantages for children, especially when used without proper guidance.  
互联网对儿童有几个明显的缺点，尤其是在没有适当指导的情况下使用。 
First, excessive screen time from using the Internet can harm their **eyes**—prolonged exposure to blue light may cause eye strain, dryness, or even affect vision development over time.  
首先，使用互联网的屏幕时间过长会损害他们的眼睛——长时间暴露在蓝光下可能会导致眼睛疲劳、干燥，甚至随着时间的推移影响视力发育。  
Second, children may easily encounter **harmful** content online, such as violent videos, inappropriate language, or misleading information. Since they lack full judgment to distinguish what is **appropriate**, this content can negatively impact their mental health, values, or sense of safety.  
其次，儿童很容易在网上遇到**有害**的内容，如暴力视频、不恰当的语言或误导性信息。由于他们缺乏充分的判断力来区分什么是**合适的**，这些内容可能会对他们的心理健康、价值观或安全感产生负面影响。  
Third, the Internet’s endless entertainment (like games or short videos) may make children **lose interest in studying**. They might get distracted by these more engaging online activities, leading to reduced focus on homework, delayed learning progress, or poor academic performance.
第三，互联网上无尽的娱乐（如游戏或短视频）可能会让孩子们对学习失去兴趣。他们可能会被这些更吸引人的在线活动分心，导致对家庭作业的关注减少、学习进度延迟或学习成绩不佳。

# 4. Do you like online shopping? why or why not?
> Words may be used:
> convenient
> compare
> cheap
> judge
> quality

Yes, I like online shopping, mainly because of its **convenient** nature—I don’t need to go out, and can browse and buy items anytime, anywhere, which saves a lot of time compared to in-store shopping.  
是的，我喜欢网上购物，主要是因为它的**方便**的性质——我不需要出去，可以随时随地浏览和购买商品，与店内购物相比节省了很多时间。  
Another reason is that it lets me easily **compare** products across different stores: I can check prices, reviews, and features of the same item without moving between shops, which often helps me find more **cheap** or cost-effective options.  
另一个原因是，它让我可以轻松地**比较**不同商店的**产品：我可以查看同一商品的价格、评论和功能，而无需在商店之间移动，这通常有助于我找到更多**便宜**或具有成本效益的选择。
Of course, there’s a small downside—I can’t touch or see the actual product, so it’s hard to directly **judge** its **quality** (like the texture of clothes or the sturdiness of a household item). But overall, the convenience and comparison benefits make online shopping a preferred choice for me.  
当然，也有一个小缺点——我无法触摸或看到实际的产品，所以很难直接**判断**它的**质量**（比如衣服的质地或家居用品的坚固性）。但总体而言，便利性和比较优势使网上购物成为我的首选。

# 5. Is online learning beneficial for students?
> Words may be used:
> whenever/wherever
> suitable
> individual
> review
> at sb's own pace
Yes, online learning is generally beneficial for students, as it addresses many limitations of traditional in-person learning and caters to diverse needs.  
是的，在线学习通常对学生有益，因为它解决了传统面对面学习的许多局限性，并满足了多样化的需求。  


First, it offers the flexibility to learn **whenever/wherever**—students don’t have to stick to fixed classroom schedules or locations, which is especially **suitable** for those with part-time jobs, family responsibilities, or who live far from schools. This flexibility eliminates time wasted on commuting and lets students arrange study around their own lives.  
首先，它提供了随时随地学习的灵活性——学生不必坚持固定的课堂时间表或地点，这尤其适合那些有兼职工作、家庭责任或远离学校的人。这种灵活性消除了通勤上浪费的时间，让学生可以围绕自己的生活安排学习。  


Second, it supports **individual** learning needs. Unlike traditional classes where teachers follow a unified pace, online learning allows students to study **at their own pace**: they can spend more time on difficult topics and speed through what they already understand. Additionally, most online courses let students easily **review** lessons (by replaying videos or revisiting materials) to reinforce knowledge—something that’s harder to do in a one-time in-person class.  
其次，它支持**个人**的学习需求。与教师遵循统一节奏的传统课程不同，在线学习允许学生按照自己的节奏学习：他们可以花更多的时间在困难的话题上，并加快他们已经理解的内容。此外，大多数在线课程让学生可以轻松地**复习**课程（通过回放视频或复习材料）来强化知识——这在一次性面对面课程中很难做到。  


While online learning requires self-discipline, its ability to adapt to individual schedules and learning styles makes it a valuable tool for students.  
虽然在线学习需要自律，但它适应个人时间表和学习风格的能力使其成为学生的宝贵工具。

# 6. What do you think about online dating?

Online dating is a double-edged sword that has reshaped how people connect romantically, with both notable advantages and important caveats.  
网上约会是一把双刃剑，它重塑了人们的浪漫关系，既有显著的优势，也有重要的警告。  

On the positive side, it breaks down traditional barriers to meeting potential partners. Unlike in-person interactions, which are often limited to one’s immediate social circle, workplace, or local community, online dating opens doors to connecting with people from different backgrounds, regions, or even countries—expanding the pool of possible matches far beyond what would be feasible offline. This is especially helpful for those with busy schedules, introverted personalities, or who live in areas with small social scenes, as it provides a low-pressure way to initiate conversations and get to know someone before meeting face-to-face.  
从积极的一面来看，它打破了与潜在合作伙伴会面的传统障碍。与通常仅限于一个人的直接社交圈、工作场所或当地社区的面对面互动不同，在线约会为与来自不同背景、地区甚至国家的人建立联系打开了大门——扩大了可能的匹配范围，远远超出了线下的可行范围。这对那些日程繁忙、性格内向或生活在社交场景较小的地区的人尤其有帮助，因为它提供了一种在面对面交流之前发起对话和了解某人的低压方式。  

However, online dating also comes with risks and downsides. One key challenge is the gap between **online personas** and real-life identities: some users may exaggerate (or even fabricate) details about themselves—such as their age, occupation, or relationship goals—to make a better first impression. This can lead to disappointment, mistrust, or wasted time when expectations don’t align with reality. Additionally, there are safety concerns: meeting someone from the internet for the first time carries risks of harm, so it requires caution (like choosing public meeting spots and sharing plans with others). There’s also the potential for **overwhelm**: the abundance of matches can lead to a “shopping mindset,” where people prioritize superficial traits (like photos) over deeper compatibility, or struggle to commit to getting to know one person thoroughly.  
然而，网上约会也有风险和缺点。一个关键的挑战是**在线角色**和现实生活中的身份之间的差距：一些用户可能会夸大（甚至捏造）自己的细节，比如他们的年龄、职业或关系目标，以给人留下更好的第一印象。当期望与现实不符时，这可能会导致失望、不信任或浪费时间。此外，还有安全问题：第一次与互联网上的人见面有受伤的风险，因此需要谨慎（比如选择公共聚会地点和与他人分享计划）。还有一种潜在的“压倒性”的可能性：大量的匹配可能会导致“购物心态”，即人们优先考虑肤浅的特征（如照片）而不是更深层次的兼容性，或者很难承诺彻底了解一个人。  

Ultimately, online dating is neither inherently good nor bad—it depends on how users approach it. With careful judgment, clear communication about intentions, and attention to safety, it can be a useful tool for finding meaningful connections. But it also requires awareness of its limitations to avoid frustration or risk.
最终，网上约会既不是天生的好也不是天生的坏——这取决于用户如何处理它。通过谨慎的判断、明确的意图沟通和对安全的关注，它可以成为寻找有意义联系的有用工具。但它也需要意识到自己的局限性，以避免沮丧或风险。

# 7. How does the Internet affect our social relationships? Please provide a brief answer.
The Internet has a dual impact on social relationships. On one hand, it strengthens connections: it lets people chat, video-call, or share life moments with loved ones **around the world** easily, bridging distance. On the other hand, it can weaken bonds: excessive online time may make people prioritize virtual interactions over in-person ones, leading to less deep, face-to-face communication—some even **lose interest in** real-life social gatherings. It also risks superficial relationships, as online chats lack the nuance of in-person cues (like body language), making it harder to build true trust.
互联网对社会关系有双重影响。一方面，它加强了联系：它让人们可以轻松地与世界各地的亲人聊天、视频通话或分享生活时刻，弥合距离。另一方面，它可能会削弱联系：过多的在线时间可能会使人们将虚拟互动置于面对面的互动之上，导致不那么深入的面对面交流——有些人甚至对现实生活中的社交聚会失去兴趣。这也可能导致肤浅的关系，因为在线聊天缺乏面对面的暗示（如肢体语言）的细微差别，这使得建立真正的信任变得更加困难。
